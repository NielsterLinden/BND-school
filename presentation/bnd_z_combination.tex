\begin{frame}{Why combining gains nothing --- and that is the result}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.56\textwidth}
\vspace{-2mm}
\fig[\textwidth]{precision_budget.pdf}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}\small
$Z\to\mm$ alone: $1941 \pm 35\pb$.\\
Combined: $1940 \pm 35\pb$.\\
Improvement: \alert{0.13\,\%}.

\medskip
\textbf{Why.} \SI{24}{pb} of the \SI{35}{pb} is the luminosity: identical normtag value, identical
runs, identical $\pm1.2\,\%$ in both channels. A fully correlated uncertainty cannot be combined
away. Add the shared theory terms and the floor is \SI{29}{pb}.

\medskip
A third channel of the same precision would not help either. The route to a better $Z$ cross
section from this dataset is a \alert{better luminosity calibration}, not more channels.

\medskip
{\footnotesize Data statistics contribute \SI{0.6}{pb} -- $0.03\,\%$. This measurement was never
going to be limited by the number of collisions.}
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}\footnotesize
The combination \emph{assumes} universality, so it cannot test it. The ratio can --- and in the

\section{3.\quad The two channels}

\begin{frame}{$Z\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}$: a 1.8\,\% measurement with 10 million events}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}\footnotesize
\textbf{Selection.} \texttt{HLT\_IsoMu24 || HLT\_IsoTkMu24}; exactly two tight, isolated,
opposite-sign muons, $p_{T} > 26/20\,$GeV, $|\eta| < 2.4$, $60 < m(\mm) < \SI{120}{GeV}$; FSR
photons recovered from the \texttt{FsrPhoton} collection.

\medskip
\textbf{Corrections, all measured in this data.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item ID / isolation / trigger scale factors from tag-and-probe \emph{fits} on the $Z$ peak
      (20 M pairs), with trigger-object matching
\item pileup weights from the Open Data luminosity table, L1-prefiring weights
\item $Z$-peak muon momentum calibration per $|\eta|$ bin
\item non-prompt muons from a same-sign fake factor (3.8 k events, 0.04\,\% of the yield)
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\textbf{Backgrounds} are tiny: 69 k of 10.4 M events (0.66\,\%), dominated by $t\bar t$ (32 k) and
$Z\to\tau\tau$ (11 k).
\end{column}

\begin{frame}{$Z\to\mu\mu$: which number to take --- the channel's own review changed it}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
\vspace{-3mm}
\fig[\textwidth]{mumu_fit_stability.pdf}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}\footnotesize
The channel's headline is a 30-bin shape fit, $\muZ = 0.9903 \pm 0.0140$. Its own review
(\src{z-mumu/REVIEW.md}, F3) then showed the fit is \alert{not robust}: \muZ{} moves over
$0.990$--$1.006$ with the binning and with whether the \texttt{SigModel} template is in.

\medskip
Only the nominal configuration has an acceptable goodness of fit, and it gets there by pulling
\texttt{SigModel} $0.5\sigma$ and constraining it to 10\,\% of its prior --- while that template
is itself flawed (the powheg sample has a generator cut at $m_{\mathrm{LHE}} < \SI{120}{GeV}$,
depleting its two highest bins by 10\,\% and 33\,\%).

\medskip
\textbf{We follow the review's recommendation:} take the \alert{counting extraction}
($\sigma_{\text{fid}} = \SI{794.4}{pb}$, $\muZ = 0.9935$) and add $\pm0.7\,\%$ as a lineshape
systematic. Using the shape fit instead moves the combination by $-\SI{6.5}{pb}$ --- reported as a
variation, not used.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{$Z\to\tauh\tauh$: a 16\,\% measurement where 81\,\% of the data is background}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}\footnotesize
\textbf{Selection.} Double-$\tauh$ trigger (different path in G and H), both legs HLT-matched;
two opposite-sign \tauh{} with $p_{T} > \SI{40}{GeV}$, $|\eta| < 2.1$, decay modes 0/1/10/11,

$\Rightarrow$ 47 586 events, of which \alert{$\sim$81\,\% are jets faking a \tauh}.

\medskip
\textbf{No QCD simulation is used.} The fake background comes from data: a fake factor
$\mathrm{FF}(\text{era}, \mathrm{DM}, N_{\text{jets}}, p_{T})$ measured in a same-sign region and
applied to opposite-sign events whose leading \tauh{} fails Medium, times an OS/SS correction
of 1.07--1.13 from the anti-isolated sideband.

\medskip
\textbf{Mass.} The visible di-$\tau$ mass is not a $Z$ mass. A per-event likelihood over the
$\tau$ visible-energy fractions using the MET covariance gives $m_{\tau\tau}$ with scale 0.99 and
resolution 11\,\% (against 0.80 / 13\,\% for $m_{\text{vis}}$).

\section{4.\quad Combination}

\begin{frame}{What is combined, and how}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}\small
\textbf{The observable.} $\sigll$ per lepton flavour, \emph{assuming lepton universality}:
\[ \sigma_{c} = \hat\mu_{c}\times\sigma^{\text{pred}}_{c}(60\text{--}120) \]

\textbf{Not} $\muZ$ itself: the two channels normalise to references \SI{9}{pb} apart for the same
quantity (backup), so averaging the $\mu$'s would average two different things.

\medskip
\textbf{The covariance}, source by source,
\[ C = \sum_{s} C_{s},\qquad (C_{s})_{ij} = \rho_{s}\,s_{i}s_{j} \]
then standard BLUE,
\[ w = \frac{C^{-1}u}{u^{T}C^{-1}u},\quad \hat\sigma = w^{T}x,\quad
   \mathrm{Var} = \frac{1}{u^{T}C^{-1}u} \]
with $\chi^{2}$ (ndf\,=\,1) for compatibility, and the combined uncertainty decomposed as
$\delta_{s} = \sqrt{w^{T}C_{s}w}$.
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}\small
\textbf{Two details that are easy to get wrong.}

\smallskip
\emph{In-fit systematics scale with the prediction}, because \muZ{} multiplies a fixed number:
$\delta\sigma = s_{\text{group}}\times\sigma^{\text{pred}}$. \emph{The acceptance is
multiplicative}, $\delta\sigma = a\times\sigma^{\text{meas}}$, since $\sigma = \sigma_{\text{fid}}/A$
--- and $A$ is outside both likelihoods (0.61\,\% for $\mu\mu$, 3.7\,\% for $\tau\tau$), so the
combination has to add it.

\smallskip
Because the acceptance is multiplicative, the BLUE is \emph{iterated}: the terms are re-evaluated
at the combined value until stable (4 iterations, $<\SI{0.1}{pb}$).

\medskip
\textbf{Cross-checked} against an explicit profile likelihood with one nuisance per shared source,
\[ -2\ln L = \sum_{c}\frac{(x_{c}-\sigma-\sum_{s}d_{cs}\theta_{s})^{2}}{u_{c}^{2}}
   + \sum_{s}\theta_{s}^{2} \]
which additionally carries the $\tau\tau$ channel's asymmetric error. Same answer:
$1939\,^{+35}_{-35}$ against $1940 \pm 35\pb$.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\textbf{$\rho = 1$ --- derived once, used by both}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item \texttt{Luminosity}: same normtag value, same runs, same $\pm1.2\,\%$
\item \texttt{Pileup}, \texttt{L1 prefiring}: same profile, same maps, same code
\item \texttt{Background normalisation}: literally the same \texttt{XS\_*} parameters on the same
      MC samples
\item PDF, $\alpha_{s}$, QCD scale, PS: same Hessian members and the same 7-point envelope of the
      \emph{same} aMC@NLO sample
\end{itemize}

\smallskip
\textbf{$\rho = 0$ --- different objects, methods or events}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\item muon vs $\tauh$ calibrations: no common input
\item \texttt{Gammas}: same sample, but $\mu\mu$ and \thth{} select disjoint events
\item fakes, MET, the $\mu\mu$ lineshape term
\item data statistics: \emph{disjoint} primary datasets, orthogonal triggers
\end{itemize}

\smallskip
\textbf{The one genuine split.} Both channels call their generator systematic
\texttt{SigModel}, so a MultiFit would correlate them by name --- but $\mu\mu$ compares two NLO
generators (0.2\,\% on $C$) and $\tau\tau$ compares LO with NLO (7.3\,\%). We split the category
by the ranking fraction and decorrelate that part.

\smallskip
$\Rightarrow$ $\rho(\mu\mu,\tau\tau) = \mathbf{0.15}$.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\item The combination is \textbf{luminosity-limited}: \SI{24}{pb} of the \SI{35}{pb}, fully

\item Data statistics are \SI{0.6}{pb}. \textbf{More collisions would change nothing};
      better calibration and more simulated events would.
\item What the $\tau\tau$ channel really contributes is the \textbf{universality test}, where the
      luminosity cancels exactly.
\item The shared conventions made this possible at all --- and the places where they were
      ambiguous (\texttt{SigModel}, the reference cross section) are exactly where the work went.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{frame}[noframenumbering]{Backup: the two references differ by 0.47\,\%}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}\small
Both channels define \muZ{} as a multiplier on an aMC@NLO prediction of
$\sigll$ --- but they build it differently:
\begin{center}\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{3.6cm}r@{}}
\toprule
channel & construction & value \\ \midrule

         $= 799.566/0.409209$ & \SI{1953.9}{pb} \\

         & \SI{1944.9}{pb} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
With 72 M generated events the statistical precision on either is 0.02\,\%, so the 0.47\,\% is
\textbf{definitional}, not a fluctuation.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}\small
\textbf{Why it matters.}
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item We combine \emph{cross sections}: each $\hat\mu$ is multiplied by its own reference first,
      so the mismatch shows up as a band on the theory line rather than as a bias.
\item A TRExFitter MultiFit with one shared \texttt{mu\_Z} would \emph{not} handle it --- it would
      fit one parameter against two references.
\item Size today: the $\tau\tau$ weight is 0.5\,\%, so $\sim$0.002\,\% on the combination. It
      matters as soon as a second channel of comparable weight appears.
\end{itemize}
Recorded as a decision in \src{fitting/CONVENTIONS.md} \S6.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}
